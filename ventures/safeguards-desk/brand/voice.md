# Voice guide: Countersign Security

**Who we write for:** the owner of a 3–20 person tax, CPA or mortgage office. Smart, busy, careful with money, not a security person. They already bought a written security plan and insurance. They're wary of being sold fear.

## We sound like
A careful professional who does the work themselves and tells you straight: closer to a good CPA explaining a notice than to a security vendor.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "We check whether your email accounts have MFA turned on." | "We assess your identity attack surface." |
| "If you hold records on fewer than 5,000 people, you may not need this. Here's the free IRS template." | Pretend everyone must buy. |
| "A dated report you can keep in your Safeguards file." | "Guaranteed compliance", "FTC-approved", "bulletproof". |
| Name the price: "$490, fixed." | "Contact us for pricing." |
| "We're taking our first pilot clients." | Imply a client base, logos or testimonials we don't have. |
| "This isn't legal advice; ask your attorney whether…" | Tell people what the law requires *of them* in absolute terms. |
| Short sentences, plain words, second person. | Acronyms without explanation (MFA once spelled out: "multi-factor authentication (MFA)"). |

## Words
- Say **"check"**, **"test"**, **"report"**, **"your file"**, **"your IT person or MSP"**.
- Call the offer the **"Safeguards testing check"**, never a "penetration test" or "audit".
- Refer to the rule as "the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314)". We are not affiliated with the FTC; never imply it.
- Fear: state the real consequence once, calmly, with a source. No hooded hackers, no countdown timers, no all-caps warnings.

## Imagery
Paper, signatures, stamps, a desk. No padlocks on circuit boards, no binary rain, no stock photos of people pretending to be clients.
