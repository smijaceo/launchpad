## Value proposition

A fixed-fee $490 check that tests what your security plan only describes: an external vulnerability scan and a read-only Microsoft 365 review, with a dated report for your Safeguards file.

## Ideal customer

The owner or managing partner of a US tax, bookkeeping/CPA or mortgage-broker office with 3–20 staff on Microsoft 365, no in-house security person, and customer records on 5,000 or more consumers (for example several years of returns or loan files). They already have, or know they need, a written information security plan, and usually have an outside IT person or MSP. A second buyer is the owner of an MSP that serves these offices and has no security-testing staff.

**Qualifies if:**
- Keeps customer information on roughly 5,000 or more consumers, counting past years, or isn't sure.
- Runs on a Microsoft 365 tenant the office controls, or whose MSP will consent to a read-only review.
- Has no dated vulnerability assessment or penetration test on file from the last 12 months.
- The owner can sign a testing authorisation and can get consent for any in-scope asset the office doesn't own.

**Not a fit if:**
- Clearly under 5,000 consumers and only needs the plan document: point them to the free IRS template, honestly.
- Already has a current pen test and vulnerability assessment from a provider.
- A tax office inside the filing-season crunch: schedule after the deadline instead.

## Top three pains

1. **Above 5,000 consumers, the Safeguards Rule asks for more than a plan document: testing on a schedule, which a template WISP doesn't do.**
   - Source wording (as summarised in the brief): continuous monitoring or annual penetration tests plus six-monthly vulnerability assessments ([source](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-314/section-314.4), regulation)
   - Note: A legal duty on paper. Whether owners feel it, or know they are above the 5,000 line, is unproven; the test asks.
2. **Security help is built and priced for bigger companies.**
   - Source wording (as summarised in the brief): providers have gone 'full-enterprise' … services that are not enterprise-focused ([source](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25520219), proxy buyer)
   - Note: A small-company operator, not a tax or mortgage owner. No named-buyer evidence exists yet.
3. **Nobody independent has checked the email and cloud accounts where a small office actually gets taken over.**
   - Source wording (as summarised in the brief): wants a professional assessment of its online accounts (email, cloud documents) so they cannot be hijacked ([source](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16255598), proxy buyer)
   - Note: A ~10-person organisation, a close proxy for the office size; still not the named buyer.

## Offer

### Safeguards testing check — $490 fixed fee

For: Owners of US tax, CPA and mortgage offices (3–20 staff, Microsoft 365), especially those with records on 5,000+ consumers, where 16 CFR 314.4(d)(2) calls for annual penetration testing and six-monthly vulnerability assessments unless the firm has effective continuous monitoring.

Includes:
- Scoping call: consumer count, systems, who owns what, and which Safeguards duties apply to the office (with an honest answer if it's under 5,000).
- Manual external vulnerability scan of internet-facing services the office owns or has written consent to test.
- Read-only Microsoft 365 review by hand: MFA coverage, admin roles, external forwarding and inbox rules, leaver and inactive accounts, security defaults or conditional access.
- Dated written report with findings ranked by risk and fix guidance for the office's IT person or MSP.
- A 30-minute walkthrough call.

Not included:
- A full penetration test, internal network, endpoint or phishing testing.
- Fixing findings (the office's IT person or MSP does that).
- Legal advice or a determination that the office is compliant.
- Testing any asset whose owner hasn't consented in writing.
- Ongoing monitoring after the report.

Before work starts:
- Signed scope and testing authorisation (template reviewed by a lawyer).
- Written consent from the host, MSP or ISP for any in-scope asset the office doesn't own.
- Temporary read-only Microsoft 365 access granted by the client and revoked at delivery.
- For tax offices, scheduled away from filing deadlines.

Call to action: **Book a 20-minute scoping call**

### MSP white-label pilot — Wholesale, about 30% below list (exact pilot terms set by the founder)

For: MSPs that serve accounting or mortgage offices and have no security-testing staff.

Includes:
- The Safeguards testing check delivered for the MSP's client offices under the MSP's brand.
- Findings owned by the client office, shared with the MSP only with the client's consent.
- Fix guidance the MSP can turn into billable remediation work.

Not included:
- Grading or replacing the MSP's own tools or monitoring.
- Testing without each client office's signed authorisation.

Before work starts:
- MSP partner agreement (lawyer-reviewed) and each client's signed authorisation.

Call to action: **Talk about a pilot (a letter of intent covering 10+ offices)**

## Validation test (from the brief, unchanged)

- **Assumption:** Small financial offices above 5,000 consumers, or MSPs on their behalf, will pay about $2k/year for verified configuration and 314.4(d)(2) testing on top of the WISP and cyber insurance they already buy.
- **Test:** In two weeks, without building software: offer 15 MSPs that serve accounting or mortgage offices a white-label pilot, and email 40 mortgage brokers and established tax offices a fixed-fee $490 'Safeguards testing check' (manual external scan plus a read-only M365 review delivered by hand). In 10 owner calls, ask about consumer count, insurer questionnaires and whether anyone has asked for proof of testing.
- **Success signal:** At least 3 paid $490 checks or 1 MSP letter of intent covering 10+ client offices, and at least 4 of 10 owners above 5,000 consumers saying they have no annual test or vulnerability assessment and would pay to have one.
