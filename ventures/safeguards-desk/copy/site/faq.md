# Questions about the Safeguards testing check

These answers cover scope, price, who the rule applies to, managed service providers (MSPs), hosted software and your data. For anything not covered here, book a scoping call.

## Is the check a penetration test?
No. The check is one external vulnerability scan plus a read-only review of your Microsoft 365 settings, so it doesn't cover the rule's annual penetration test. It's called a testing check so nobody mistakes it for a full penetration test. If you need a penetration test, raise it on the scoping call.

## Does the check make my office compliant with the Safeguards Rule?
No check can promise that. The report is evidence of what was checked, on what date, and what was found. Your attorney decides whether your overall program meets the rule.

## Does the Safeguards Rule apply to my office?
The rule gives tax preparation services, accountants in the business of completing income tax returns, and mortgage brokers as examples of financial institutions, so if your office does that work it likely applies; your attorney confirms. <!--c:fsr-covers--> Its testing schedule, annual penetration testing and vulnerability assessments at least every six months, doesn't apply to offices that hold customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers. <!--c:fsr-5000--> <!--c:fsr-testing-->

## My office is under 5,000 consumers. Should I buy the check?
Not to meet the rule's testing schedule, which doesn't apply to you. <!--c:fsr-5000--> The rule still asks you to regularly test or otherwise monitor your key controls, and how you do that is your choice. The Internal Revenue Service's (IRS) free Publication 5708 helps you write the plan itself. <!--c:irs-5708--> Some smaller offices still want an outside look at their email setup.

## My MSP already monitors my office. Do I still need the check?
Possibly not. The rule accepts effective continuous monitoring as an alternative to the testing schedule. <!--c:fsr-testing--> Ask your MSP what they monitor and whether they can give you a dated record. If they want help with the testing side, point them to the [MSP pilot](/msp/).

## My tax software runs on a hosted desktop. Can it be tested?
Only with the hosting provider's written permission. Without it, the hosted system stays out of scope and the report says so.

## Do you need my passwords?
No. Your Microsoft 365 administrator assigns a read-only role to a dedicated account for the check and removes it at delivery. Countersign Security doesn't open or read your messages. The review does read mailbox settings such as inbox and forwarding rules, which can include sender addresses and keywords, and reports only what matters to a finding. <!--c:no-message-reading-->

## What does the check cost?
A fixed $490. <!--c:price-490--> If you want the check repeated on a schedule, raise it on the call.

## How long does the check take?
You agree a testing window on the scoping call, once the authorisation is signed and around your filing calendar. The report arrives within 5 business days of the window closing.

## What happens if the check finds signs of an intrusion?
You hear about it the same day, not at report time. Call your cyber insurer's hotline or your attorney first: they decide what has to be reported and to whom. For reference, the rule requires notice to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) as soon as possible, and no later than 30 days after discovery, of certain events involving the information of 500 or more consumers. <!--c:fsr-500-->
